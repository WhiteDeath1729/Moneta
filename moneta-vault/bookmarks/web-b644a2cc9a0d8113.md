Overnight, he was confronted by a force far greater than his own and the office he now holds. The US president ran headlong into a brick wall of opposition and had no option but to back down. It''s called the bond market — an arena where brutality knows no bounds and where the participants take no prisoners. Stock market live updates: Follow the ASX reaction to Trump''s tariff u-turn in our finance blog Just hours earlier, at a black-tie event, the president delivered another rambling speech where he belittled world leaders and basked in the glow of his own exalted position. "I''m telling you, these countries are calling us up, kissing my ass," he told the National Republican Congressional Committee''s annual dinner in Washington. "They are. They are dying to make a deal. " Please, please, sir, make a deal. I''ll do anything. I''ll do anything, sir! " As markets tanked, Trump arrived at a fork in the road Perched on one shoulder, the demons have been appealing to Donald Trump''s long-held love of tariffs. On the other side, the angels have been appealing to his long-held love of deal-making. Hours later, it was Mr Trump who capitulated, calling a 90 day reprieve on his tariff war and slashing the tax to just 10 per cent for all nations except China. For weeks, he has shrugged aside an unrelenting fall on Wall Street as stocks dived on the prospect of lower growth, a spike in inflation and the growing chance of a recession. But on Tuesday, after weeks of discontent, the real power players on financial markets took action. In an unprecedented attack on America''s global financial dominance, they began abandoning US government bonds, sending market interest rates sharply higher. More than a mere rebuke, it was an ominous sign that the world was losing faith in America and that the US would pay the price. Rather than kissing, global bond markets began kicking his ass. Loading... Safe haven no more There has always been a golden rule for money managers. In times of uncertainty, whether caused by diplomatic, social or economic turbulence, head for safety. And the safest harbour of all has always been US government bonds. Money floods in, regardless of the cause of the crisis. It''s because not only is America the world''s biggest economy, it''s also considered that the greenback is the global reserve currency. Check out our tariffs topic page You know when you buy US government bonds — essentially US government debt — that you''ll be repaid. These bonds set what is known as the "risk free" global interest rate and help price interest rates across the entire international financial system. As the cash flows in, it pushes down interest rates and strengthens the greenback, creating an automatic buffer out there in the real world against the havoc taking place elsewhere in the system. This week, that mechanism stopped working. Why gold has slipped during market rout despite safe-haven status While global markets have been reeling from Donald Trump''s sweeping tariffs, gold prices remain near record highs. Bullion is currently exempt from the US import charges, as central banks including the Federal Reserve drive demand. For weeks, money flows into US bonds were unusually low given the turmoil on stock markets, to the point that the US dollar was sinking. On Tuesday, the bond market went to war with Trump and his trade war. Rather than flooding in, cash began pouring out of the system. US government bonds were being dumped and the yields, essentially the interest rates, began rising sharply. We''re only human For all the level-headed formulae and complex trading structures that make financial markets such a mystery to most, when push comes to shove, everything is reduced to basic human emotions. It starts with faith. That creates confidence. Without those key ingredients, fear takes hold. That old saying, ''my word is my bond'', is a powerful force that underpins almost all commercial transactions. Loading We''ve witnessed first-hand the fragility of the financial system when trust evaporates, when doubts emerge about its viability. During the 2008 global financial crisis, almost every institution in the developed world stopped lending. Problems that had emerged across the global network led to doubts about the extent of the problem, until finally the fear became contagious and perfectly viable institutions faced ruin because they could not conduct vital refinancing. In the space of weeks, Donald Trump has up-ended a trading regime his country designed and championed, and which took 80 years to build. For now, he''s been forced to retreat. Should he continue, something is almost certain to break. Loading...
